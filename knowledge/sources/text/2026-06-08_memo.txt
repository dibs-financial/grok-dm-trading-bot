CRYPTO
Bitcoin (BTC)
BTC trades near $62,500, approximately 50% off the October
2025 all-time high of $126,198. The $60,000 level is the critical
support zone; below that, $55,000-$58,000 is the next reference
area. The 200-day MA has been declining since early May,
acting as overhead resistance alongside the 50-day MA.
On-chain: funding rates on perpetuals are neutral to slightly
negative, indicating leveraged speculation has partially flushed.
MVRV has not yet reached historically reliable accumulation
territory. ETF-driven outflows (largest of 2026 at $1.67B weekly)
are creating direct sell pressure; a reversal of that flow would be
a meaningful near-term catalyst.
Ethereum (ETH)
ETH is trading around $1,700-$1,800, down roughly 29% in Q1
2026 alone and continuing to underperform BTC on this
correction. Market cap is approximately $230-235B. The
Glamsterdam upgrade is on the roadmap but subject to timeline
adjustments; do not treat it as a near-term price catalyst without
confirmation. Layer-2 adoption and DeFi fee revenues remain
structurally intact.
Hyperliquid (HYPE): Cycle Outlier
HYPE recently printed an all-time high, one of the only assets to
do so during a period of extreme fear. The decentralized
perpetuals exchange is processing billions in daily open interest
with real volume and fee revenue. ETF products from Bitwise,
21Shares, and Grayscale have launched around the
ecosystem.
Current support: ~$56. Secondary accumulation zone: $38-$44
if broader market pressure continues. ATH during extreme fear
is a meaningful divergence signal.
Altcoin Landscape
Speculative altcoins broadly under pressure; capital
concentrating in higher-quality assets. Notable watches: AI
tokens (Bittensor, Render, Venice) approaching historical
accumulation zones; RWA tokens (Ondo, Canton) holding
relatively better on the tokenization thesis. Position sizing
matters more than narrative selection in this environment.
MACRO
May NFP: 172K vs. 85K Expected
Friday's nonfarm payrolls report was a blowout: 172,000 jobs added
against a consensus of 85,000. Unemployment held at 4.3%; wage
growth 3.4% YoY in line. A labor market this resilient, combined with
elevated oil-driven inflation, removes any near-term justification for
Fed rate cuts. June hold probability: 99.4%. The more significant
shift: markets are now pricing a ~73% chance of a rate hike by
year-end.
Equities: Nasdaq -4.18% on Friday
The Nasdaq fell 4.18% on Friday (worst day since April 2025); S&P;
500 -2.64% to 7,383; Dow -695 points. Catalyst: semiconductor
sector breakdown after Broadcom failed to raise its AI chip outlook
Wednesday night. NFP-driven treasury yield spike accelerated the
selloff. VIX +39.68%. The S&P; had briefly crossed 7,600 earlier in
the week on chip/AI enthusiasm.
Oil and Geopolitics
WTI crude: ~$90-94/bbl. Brent: ~$96-97/bbl. Down from the April
peak of $138 but still historically elevated. The near-closure of the
Strait of Hormuz and ~10.5M b/d of forced Middle Eastern shut-ins in
April created a supply shock the market is still absorbing. Saudi
Aramco CEO: full normalization takes until 2027 even if the Strait
reopens today.
Geopolitical status: Iran launched missiles toward Israel over the
weekend (all intercepted). Hezbollah rejected ceasefire. 60-day
truce framework stalled. OPEC+ approved +188,000 b/d July quota
increase. UAE departed OPEC May 1, reducing cartel spare
capacity from 3.8M to 2.5M b/d. EIA STEO update due June 9.
Gold and Dollar
Gold dropped to $4,370/oz on Friday, the 2026 YTD low, on a -3.8%
weekly decline. The NFP-driven treasury yield spike raised the
opportunity cost of holding gold. Institutional year-end targets remain
$5,400-$6,300 but near-term path is lower while rate hike
expectations persist. USD Index at ~100, firm but range-bound. A
stronger dollar creates direct headwinds for crypto and risk assets
broadly.
