READING THE DATA — AND THE IMPORTANT CAVEAT
Core 2.5% is the lowest since March 2021 — genuine progress
The core reading was helped by shelter softening to +0.1% MoM. Energy fell 1.5% MoM. Both suggest the July environment was genuinely disinflationary.
The bond market is not fully convinced — 10-yr at 4.70%
Even as hike odds fell and stocks rallied, the 10-year yield stayed near a 19-month high. The long end is pricing structurally higher inflation, not near-term Fed cuts.
The critical caveat: July data is already a month old
This report reflects July oil prices — which benefited from Hormuz diplomatic relief mid-month. The Iran-US MOU expires today. August oil trajectory determines whether this 
disinflationary read persists.

A Hormuz escalation pushing Brent above $92, or a hot August CPI (due ~Sep 10-12), could flip September back to a live hike meeting. The relief is real but fragile.

WHAT IT MEANS
The online decline is partly payback from June's early Prime Day. The structural 
weakness is in autos — a rate-sensitive category that reacts to elevated financing 
costs.
Consumer spending is rotating from goods to services (restaurants +0.5%, fourth 
straight). That's not a recession pattern — it's a post-reopening normalization that 
slowed.
Michigan sentiment at 51 (-8%) with inflation expectations above 4% for the fifth 
straight month suggests consumers feel squeezed between sticky inflation and high 
rates.
For the Fed, a softening consumer reduces the case for hiking. For equities, it raises 
questions about whether the growth narrative can sustain current valuations.
KEY QUESTION: Is the consumer softening a temporary dip (payback from Prime Day) or the start of a structural deceleration? August retail sales (Sep) will answer that.

THE PUZZLE: WHY DIDN'T SOFT CPI MOVE BITCOIN?
Three soft prints materialized: CPI in-line, PPI flat, retail sales -0.6%. S&P hit a new ATH. September hike odds fell from 44% to 31%. Bitcoin went from $65,000 to $63,100. The 
bull case played out macro-side, and BTC still fell.
The answer: in-line CPI wasn't a positive surprise. The NFP miss two weeks ago already moved hike odds from 57% to 44%. The additional move from 44% to 31% on a consensus 
print is incremental — not the catalyst needed to break the $65,600 EMA ceiling that has held all year.
ETF flows confirm the diagnosis. The week of Aug 3-7 produced $853M — the best week since April. Last week (Aug 10-14) went net negative. Aug 10 alone: -$144.7M. The 
institutional re-entry that was building for 4 weeks stalled and reversed.
Strategy sold 1,690 more BTC (~$108M) last week — the seventh consecutive week of BTC sales with no purchases. When the world's largest corporate Bitcoin holder is a 
persistent net seller, it suppresses the price ceiling that ETF inflows need to push through.
