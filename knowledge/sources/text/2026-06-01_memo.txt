CRYPTO OUTLOOK
Crypto closed May in the red, diverging sharply from equities. The Fear and Greed Index sits at 28 (Fear). A confluence of macro headwinds,
institutional repositioning, and thin liquidity created a difficult tape across digital assets last week, breaking what is historically a strong month for
Bitcoin.
BITCOIN
ETF Outflows: Ten consecutive days of net outflows from U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs. AUM contracted from $104B to $94B. YTD inflows collapsed to
$1.2B from $3.9B two weeks ago. A $1.26B block sale of BlackRock IBIT on May 26 at a 2.3% discount to NAV was characterized by NYDIG as a
large investor rapid exit, not a mechanical basis-trade unwind. That distinction is a sentiment signal.
On-Chain Divergence: Whale accumulation added roughly 270,000 BTC over April-May. Exchange supply continues trending lower, historically a
precursor to supply tightening. Long-term holder behavior remains intact. The distribution picture does not confirm the ETF outflow narrative,
suggesting two distinct participant types making two distinct decisions.
Technical: Consolidating the $73-77K range. The 50-day MA is above price and declining (resistance). The 200-day MA has been falling since May 1.
Key support: $73-75K. A sustained break below $73K opens a more meaningful downside case.
ETHEREUM
Price Structure: ETH hovers near $2,000 with RSI near 33 (daily oversold). The 50-day EMA at $2,133 is immediate resistance. Sustained ETF
outflows throughout May have removed institutional buy-side pressure. Bears dominate the current chart structure; analysts have flagged $1,850 as
the next key support.
Glamsterdam Upgrade (H1 2026): Key components include EIP-7928 (block-level access lists enabling parallel transaction processing) and EIP-7732
(enshrined Proposer-Builder Separation). Gas limit target: beyond 100M. Substantive infrastructure work, but not an immediate price catalyst.
Setup: Whale accumulation building below $2,500 creates clustered liquidity with short squeeze potential on a positive macro catalyst. Downside:
$1,850. Upside on relief: $2,500+.
RELATIVE STRENGTH
HYPE and XLM are outperforming the broader crypto market this morning while BTC and ETH are red. HYPE, anchored to the Hyperliquid perpetuals
ecosystem, continues to diverge positively from the broader tape. In a weak market, names that hold bid while others sell indicate where real demand
is concentrated.

MACRO OUTLOOK
IRAN, OIL, AND THE STRAIT OF HORMUZ
The US-Iran conflict, ongoing since February 28, remains the dominant macro driver of 2026. Iran closed the Strait of Hormuz following U.S. and
Israeli airstrikes, disrupting roughly 25% of global seaborne oil trade and 20% of LNG flows. Brent spiked from the low-$60s in January to above $116
in early May.
Last Week: A tentative MoU framework was reached Thursday, briefly lifting sentiment and sending oil lower. Friday, Trump issued new demands
covering full Strait reopening, a halt to Iran's nuclear program, and unfreezing of Iranian assets. Tehran pushed back. The deal is unresolved. Brent is
now near $93 (WTI $88). Separately, Treasury Secretary Bessent disclosed the U.S. seized approximately $1B in Iranian cryptocurrency.
Binary Risk: A credible deal sends Brent toward $80-85, easing PCE and reopening the rate cut conversation. A breakdown pushes Brent back toward
$100+, reinforcing rate hike probability (currently ~46% by December). Single headlines can swing Brent 15-20 dollars.
GOLD
Holding above $4,500 this morning. Gold retreated briefly on tentative Iran deal optimism but did not break lower, signaling persistent structural
demand. Annual PCE at 3.8%, ongoing global central bank accumulation, and a dollar at DXY 99 all support the current price regime. Primary risk: a
durable comprehensive peace deal that materially improves the inflation outlook, a high bar given the current state of negotiations.
THE DOLLAR AND THE FED
DXY near 99. Last week's PCE: headline 3.8% YoY, core 3.3%, both well above the 2% target though monthly readings came in below expectations.
FOMC held at 3.50-3.75% with an 8-4 vote, the largest dissent since October 1992. Kevin Warsh takes over as Fed Chair on June 18; he is
significantly more hawkish. BofA and Goldman have pushed rate cut expectations to H2 2027.
EQUITIES
S&P 500 closed May at 7,580 (+5% month), Nasdaq gained over 8% (best month since November), Dow crossed 51,000 for the first time. Ninth
consecutive weekly gain for the S&P, the longest streak since 2023. AI capex projected at $670B in 2026; AI-related names now represent ~45% of
S&P market cap. Key risk: breadth is thin, S&P RSI entered this week at 73 (overbought), and statistically 9-week streaks tend to break in the 10th
week.
